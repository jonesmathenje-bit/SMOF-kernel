# 1. Purpose

This theorem establishes the sufficiency of the SMOF primitive vocabulary for the construction of every mathematical construct generated by the constitutionally defined SMOF construction rules.

The theorem converts the foundational assertion of PA-0001 into a theorem-level result relative to the certified SMOF construction grammar.

---

# 2. Scope

This theorem applies to every construct generated by finite application of constitutionally permitted SMOF construction rules.

It does not assert that every mathematical object in mathematics is reducible to the SMOF primitive set.

Its claim is restricted to the class of constructs admitted by the SMOF mathematical construction system.

---

# 3. Primitive Set

Let the SMOF primitive set be

\[
\mathcal{P}=\{E,O,P,R\}
\]

where:

- \(E\) denotes MathematicalEntity;
- \(O\) denotes MathematicalObject;
- \(P\) denotes MathematicalOperator;
- \(R\) denotes MathematicalRuntime.

By PA-0001, these constructs constitute the primitive vocabulary of the Certified Mathematical Foundation.

---

# 4. Construction Class

Let \(\mathcal{C}\) denote the class of all SMOF constructs generated by finite constitutional composition from the primitive set.

Thus,

\[
\mathcal{P}\subseteq\mathcal{C}.
\]

A construct \(C\in\mathcal{C}\) is generated either as:

1. a primitive construct \(p\in\mathcal{P}\); or
2. a finite composition of previously generated valid constructs.

The construction process is therefore inductive.

---

# 5. Composition Rule

Let \(C_1\) and \(C_2\) be valid SMOF constructs.

A composition

\[
C_2\circ C_1
\]

is valid whenever the codomain of \(C_1\) is compatible with the domain of \(C_2\).

In particular, where

\[
\operatorname{Cod}(C_1)
\subseteq
\operatorname{Dom}(C_2),
\]

the composition is constitutionally admissible.

For MathematicalOperators this follows from PL-0001.

---

# 6. Theorem Statement

**Theorem.**

Every construct \(C\in\mathcal{C}\) can be represented by a finite composition of primitive constructs from

\[
\mathcal{P}=\{E,O,P,R\}.
\]

Equivalently, for every

\[
C\in\mathcal{C},
\]

there exists a finite expression

\[
C=P_n\circ P_{n-1}\circ\cdots\circ P_1
\]

where each constituent \(P_i\) is generated from the primitive vocabulary

\[
\mathcal{P}.
\]

The composition is valid only where all required constitutional compatibility conditions are satisfied.

---

# 7. Proof Strategy

The proof proceeds by structural induction on the construction depth of a SMOF construct.

Define the construction depth

\[
d(C)\in\mathbb{N}
\]

as follows:

\[
d(p)=0
\]

for every primitive

\[
p\in\mathcal{P}.
\]

If

\[
C=C_2\circ C_1,
\]

then

\[
d(C)=1+\max(d(C_1),d(C_2)).
\]

---

# 8. Base Case

Let

\[
C\in\mathcal{P}.
\]

Then \(C\) is itself one of

\[
E,\ O,\ P,\ R.
\]

Therefore \(C\) is already a finite composition consisting of a single primitive construct.

Hence the theorem holds for all constructs satisfying

\[
d(C)=0.
\]

---

# 9. Inductive Hypothesis

Assume that every valid SMOF construct \(C\) satisfying

\[
d(C)\leq n
\]

can be represented by a finite composition of primitive constructs from \(\mathcal{P}\).

---

# 10. Inductive Step

Let \(C\) be a valid construct satisfying

\[
d(C)=n+1.
\]

By the definition of the SMOF construction class, \(C\) is generated by a valid finite composition

\[
C=C_2\circ C_1
\]

where

\[
d(C_1)\leq n
\]

and

\[
d(C_2)\leq n.
\]

By the inductive hypothesis, both \(C_1\) and \(C_2\) admit finite representations generated from the primitive set \(\mathcal{P}\).

Thus there exist finite primitive compositions

\[
C_1=P_k\circ\cdots\circ P_1
\]

and

\[
C_2=P_m\circ\cdots\circ P_{k+1},
\]

where every constituent primitive is derived from

\[
\mathcal{P}.
\]

Therefore,

\[
C
=
C_2\circ C_1
\]

can be expanded into the finite composition

\[
C
=
P_m\circ\cdots\circ P_{k+1}
\circ
P_k\circ\cdots\circ P_1.
\]

By PL-0001, valid operator compositions remain within the MathematicalOperator domain whenever the required domain-codomain compatibility condition is satisfied.

Therefore \(C\) is itself representable by a finite composition generated from the primitive set.

Hence the inductive step holds.

---

# 11. Conclusion of Proof

By structural induction, every construct

\[
C\in\mathcal{C}
\]

has a finite representation generated from

\[
\mathcal{P}=\{E,O,P,R\}.
\]

Therefore,

\[
\boxed{
\forall C\in\mathcal{C},
\quad
C\text{ is finitely generated from }\mathcal{P}
}
\]

subject to the constitutional composition rules and compatibility conditions.

Thus the primitive set is sufficient for the generation of the entire SMOF construction class.

\[
\boxed{\text{QED}}
\]

---

# 12. Interpretation

The theorem establishes **SMOF construction sufficiency**, not universal mathematical expressiveness.

The distinction is fundamental.

The theorem states:

\[
\mathcal{C}
\subseteq
\operatorname{Closure}(\mathcal{P})
\]

where \(\mathcal{C}\) is the class of constitutionally admitted SMOF constructs.

It does not state:

\[
\text{All mathematics}
\subseteq
\operatorname{Closure}(\mathcal{P}).
\]

The latter would require substantially stronger definitions and mathematical results.

---

# 13. Relationship to PA-0001

PA-0001 establishes the primitive basis axiomatically.

PT-0001 establishes that the primitive basis is sufficient for the constitutionally defined construction class.

Therefore:

\[
PA\text{-}0001
\Rightarrow
\text{primitive basis}
\]

while

\[
PT\text{-}0001
\Rightarrow
\text{sufficiency of that basis for }\mathcal{C}.
\]

The theorem therefore does not replace PA-0001.

It derives a consequence of the accepted primitive foundation.

---

# 14. Relationship to PL-0001

PL-0001 establishes closure of compatible operator composition.

PT-0001 uses this closure property in its inductive step.

The dependency structure is therefore:

\[
PA\text{-}0001
\rightarrow
PL\text{-}0001
\rightarrow
PT\text{-}0001.
\]

This establishes a non-circular proof dependency.

---

# 15. Consequences

PT-0001 establishes the following consequences:

1. Higher-order SMOF constructs need not be introduced as new primitives.
2. Finite construction depth is sufficient for every construct in \(\mathcal{C}\).
3. Operator composition can be used as a principal mechanism for mathematical construction.
4. The primitive vocabulary remains constitutionally minimal within the defined construction system.
5. Subsequent theorems may reason about higher-order constructs through their primitive decomposition.

---

# 16. Limitations

This theorem depends on the formal definition of the SMOF construction class \(\mathcal{C}\).

It therefore does not establish:

- completeness with respect to arbitrary mathematics;
- computational completeness;
- logical completeness;
- categorical completeness;
- semantic completeness;
- expressiveness relative to external mathematical systems.

Those claims require separate definitions and theorems.
